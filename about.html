ANDREW'S STORY / IN HIS WORDS

I built the support I wish I had.

My name is Andrew Senorans, and I founded Tutronix to be the kind of academic support I needed when I was growing up.

Start at the beginning ↓
Founder / Tutor
Miami, Florida
01

THE BEGINNING

Smart, disruptive, and difficult to categorize.

As a kid, I struggled with anxiety and ADHD. I would zone out during lectures, constantly interrupt, and I was known as a troublemaker. I was smart, but disruptive, and no one knew what to make of that.

At one point, I was tested for autism and cycled through therapists and psychologists. Teachers did not know whether to be impressed or concerned. I was not failing classes, but I was not exactly thriving either. Honestly, it is shocking that my grades were as high as they were, considering how little I could focus.

The way I learned and functioned did not match what the system expected from a “good student.”

I was a gifted child falling behind in a system that did not understand how I learned.
02

THE TEST

The youngest one in the room.

Then, in fifth grade, things got even stranger. I accidentally aced a math placement test that most students were meant to fail. So they sent me upstairs, alone, to sit in a middle school algebra class with older kids.

I was the youngest person in the room. There was no explanation, no support, and no credit. I kept up just fine, but it was clear: the school did not know what to do with me. Again.

THE PERSON WHO SAW ME

My mom listened.

She saw my love of science and enrolled me in a math and science program for grades six through twelve. In middle school, I started to shine, especially in math and science.

03

BENEATH THE GRADES

Succeeding on paper. Chaotic behind the scenes.

Even then, my grades would sometimes drop for reasons that had nothing to do with comprehension. Anxiety, ADHD, and the focus issues I had dealt with for so long would creep back in. A missed assignment here, a forgotten project there, and I would lose track of things.

No one taught me how to manage my time. No one showed me how to stay organized, initiate tasks, or stay focused when my mind was racing. That lingering chaos under the surface never fully went away, and I carried the frustration quietly for years.

  • Manage time
  • Stay organized
  • Initiate tasks
  • Sustain focus
04

BUILDING A SYSTEM

College taught me that intelligence was never the issue.

In high school, I excelled in math and science and went on to study mechanical engineering at the University of Miami. College brought a whole new level of personal responsibility: complex schedules, projects that stretched over months, relationships, and the need to stay motivated without anyone looking over my shoulder.

I learned to build systems that worked for me. I learned to plan, manage my time, and stay on top of deadlines. No one had taught me how, but I had to figure it out. Slowly, I developed the executive functioning skills I had never been explicitly taught as a child.

For the first time, I saw how much of my earlier struggle had nothing to do with intelligence and everything to do with structure.

THE TURNING POINT

The student understood. The grade still did not change.

During college, I started tutoring kids in math and science. At first, it was a way to make extra money doing something I enjoyed. Parents would reach out with familiar concerns:

My kid is struggling in algebra.They have a big test coming up.They need someone to explain it better.

I would sit with the student and break down the content. It clicked. They could solve the problems on their own. They were capable.

But test scores still did not improve. A week would pass and assignments were not turned in. Grades stayed low. Confidence stayed lower.

THAT'S WHEN IT HIT METhis was not a content problem.
It was an executive functioning problem.

WHY TUTRONIX EXISTS

Learn better.
Function better.

That was the moment Tutronix began to take shape. I wanted to combine academic tutoring with practical coaching in focus, organization, planning, and mindset. These are skills students can use long after a test is over.

Over the years, I have worked with students across a wide range of needs and abilities. Some are gifted but overwhelmed. Others have ADHD, autism, anxiety, or learning differences that make traditional schooling difficult. Many are bright students who simply have not been given the tools or support to succeed.

I understand them because I was once in their shoes. I know how powerful it is to have someone believe in you and guide you with patience, structure, and empathy.
